Our Philosophy
"Luxury and spirituality are not opposites. In India, they never were."

India's hospitality tradition doesn't come from the hotel industry. It comes from the Upanishads. Atithi Devo Bhava — the guest is God. When you stay in a palace hotel in Rajasthan or a haveli in Varanasi, you're not taking a break from the pilgrimage. You're continuing it.

The seva of a butler and the seva of a priest spring from the same source. This is why Bhakti and Bougie are not in tension. In India, they never were.

I've slept on a charpoy and stayed at the Taj Lake Palace. I've eaten at dhabas and at Tresind Studio. I've been on the bus and I've had the private boat. I know the value of all of it. When I take you to India, we do it right — because discomfort is not a prerequisite for depth.

Sacred Geography
Holy Places

India is not a backdrop. It is the destination — a subcontinent organised around the sacred, where every city, river, mountain, and crossroads has a name the gods gave it first. These are the places we return to.

Uttar Pradesh
Varanasi
The oldest continuously inhabited city on earth. The eternal city. Every Hindu hopes to die here — because Shiva himself whispers the taraka mantra into the ear of the dying.
Uttarakhand
Kedarnath
At 3,583 metres, in the ice, above the cloud line. One of the 12 jyotirlingas. Shiva's abode in the high Himalayas — a temple that closes every winter and opens again to pilgrims who have waited all year.
Tamil Nadu
Chidambaram
Where Nataraja performs the cosmic dance for eternity in a golden hall. One of the five elemental Shiva temples — the akasha lingam, the lingam of space itself.
Uttar Pradesh
Vrindavan
Where Krishna spent his childhood, danced with the gopis, stole butter, and broke every heart. Every street a temple. Every temple a love story. The dust of Vrindavan is said to be sacred.
Bihar
Bodh Gaya
The bodhi tree. The exact spot. The moment Siddhartha touched the earth and the world changed. Every Buddhist tradition on earth maintains a presence here. The silence is unlike anywhere else.
Uttarakhand
Kasar Devi Temple
Perched above Almora in the Kumaon Himalayas, within a Van Allen Belt anomaly that drew Swami Vivekananda, D.H. Lawrence, and Cat Stevens. Sacred and strange in equal measure.

Michael's love affair with India began before he could fully explain it — a pull that predated any rational justification and has never required one. Over two decades of returns, he has studied Sanskrit, been initiated into a devotional lineage, completed advanced yoga teacher training in Mysore, and learned to navigate the subcontinent with equal fluency in five-star heritage hotels and roadside chai stalls.

He holds a Master's degree in International Studies and an MBA, and spent years in business before recognizing that the work he was born to do was to help others find what India had given him: a direct encounter with the sacred, held in conditions beautiful enough that the ego stops fighting and the real journey can begin.

He works with a small number of travelers each year — people who are spiritually curious, unafraid of depth, and unwilling to compromise on comfort. He does not use templates. He does not rush. He has never led a tour he wouldn't want to be on himself.

Selfless Service
BHAKTI & BOUGIE is structured as a for-profit travel company. The charitable work below is personal — Michael's own, offered here because it is inseparable from why this company exists.

In a small Dalit village outside Jaipur, Rajasthan, Michael has been involved with a community-run girls' education centre for years. The project grew from relationships formed on early solo journeys — the kind of relationships that happen when you slow down, stay longer, and stop treating a country as a backdrop.

During the COVID lockdowns of 2020–21, when the village lost its primary income source overnight, Michael helped coordinate emergency food and supply distribution through local contacts and international donors. What had been a spiritual curiosity became an obligation of love.

The girls' education centre is now fully staffed by local teachers. It serves children who would otherwise have no formal schooling. It is the thing Michael is most proud of, and it has nothing to do with luxury — except that the luxury is what made it possible to stay long enough to care.

A portion of every booking goes toward the centre's operational costs. Travelers who wish to visit during their journey are welcome to ask.
